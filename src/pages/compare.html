Run applications, not machines.

Proxmox VE runs virtual machines and containers very well. Kubernetes runs containerised services. Apelora Fabric runs an application you drew, with its parts, their connections and their failover, on your own Linux servers.

Why not Proxmox VE?

If looking after virtual machines yourself is what you want, use Proxmox VE: it is mature and widely used. Apelora Fabric is for teams that would rather describe the application and let the platform do the rest.

  • You draw the application, not the machines: web servers, a database, a cache, and which connects to which.
  • Its network follows the drawing: the application gets its own network, and only the connections you drew are allowed.
  • Replicas sit behind one address with health checks, on different servers. A lost replica is replaced, and before a server goes into maintenance its replicas stop receiving requests.
  • Risky actions need a second person, and every change is recorded with who made it.

Apelora Fabric runs on Incus, the open source system container and virtual machine manager. It is in early development; see what works today.

When to choose another

Choose Proxmox VE when…

You want to create and look after virtual machines and containers yourself, one by one. You need a long track record today: Proxmox VE has had stable releases since 2008.

Choose Kubernetes when…

Your team builds containerised services, already knows its tools and manifests, and is ready to add the parts it leaves to you on your own servers, such as a load balancer for external addresses and a network plugin that enforces network policies.

Choose Apelora Fabric when…

You run your own Linux servers and want to run applications rather than machines: drawn once, with their network rules, service addresses, failover and approvals handled for you. It is early software, so start with a lab or a non-critical application, and talk to us about what it is ready for.
