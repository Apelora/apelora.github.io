    A security incident: a stolen credential, a misused account or a session you did not expect

    Evidence: written from the controls Apelora has. Each control is tested (API and browser tests, MOCK VERIFIED; the certificate rotation is REAL INCUS TESTED). The procedure as a whole has not been drilled and no identity provider, directory or MAAS was involved: NOT YET VERIFIED. Record the result here after a drill.

    Order of work: contain, keep the evidence, find out what was done, recover, learn. Contain first and quickly: everything below that contains is reversible, and everything is audited.

    0. Know what Apelora can and cannot tell you

    • Every change and every refusal is in the audit trail (append-only, written in the same transaction as the change). Sessions record who, which instance, when, from which address, why it ended and how many bytes moved.
    • A terminal session's content is not recorded. You can learn that someone had a shell on an instance and for how long, not what they typed. Look at what the instance itself logged (Instance → logs, or on the server) and at what changed afterwards.
    • Identity-provider tokens cannot be revoked by Apelora before they expire (at most an hour). Disable the person in Apelora to cut them off at once, and disable the account at the provider.
    • Terminal tickets and running sessions live in the control plane's memory: restarting it ends them all.
    • Legacy apl_ tokens are platform-wide credentials and are exempt from approvals. If any exist, list them (Administration) and revoke what is not needed; set APELORA_LEGACY_TOKENS=disabled once everyone uses sign-in.

    1. Contain

    Pick what fits; do several.

    What is suspectDoTakes effect
    A person's accountIdentity & Access → Users → Disable. Their Apelora sessions are revoked with it.Next request; a running terminal ends within 30 s (access-revoked)
    A person's open terminalIdentity & Access → Sessions → End, or DELETE /api/v1/sessions/{id}At once
    Someone's roleRevoke the assignment (Identity & Access → Project memberships), or remove them from the group that grants itNext request; a running terminal ends within 30 s
    An API tokenAdministration → Tokens → Revoke, or DELETE /api/v1/tokens/{id}Next request
    A person's personal token (apt_…)Their Your tokens page, or an administrator revoking it; it is evaluated live against their roles, so disabling the person also stops itNext request
    A service identity (automation)Service identities → Disable, then rotate its tokenNext request
    An emergency-access grantRevoke the assignment; grants end by themselves within 4 hoursNext request
    A sign-in methodIdentity & Access → Sign-in: turn off the identity provider, the directory, or local accounts. The server refuses a change that would lock everyone out.At once
    All terminal accessSet APELORA_CONSOLE_ACCESS=disabled and restart the control plane. This also ends every running session.On restart
    A person's pending approvalsApprovals → Cancel/RejectAt once
    An Incus certificate (deploy or admin)Rotate it: certificates.mdAfter rotation
    A servermember-removal.md or power it off and treat it as failed: member-failure.md—

    Keep at least one trusted super administrator enabled and able to sign in; Apelora refuses to disable the last enabled one.

    2. Keep the evidence

    1. Export the audit trail for the period of concern: Audit → Export (CSV or JSON lines), or GET /api/v1/audit/export?…. The export is itself audited (audit.exported). Store it outside the control plane.
    2. Support bundle: Administration → Support bundle (secrets are scrubbed; it is audited).
    3. Note request ids from anything suspicious: they join the audit row, the log line, the job and the Incus operation (troubleshooting.md).
    4. Do not delete anything to "clean up". Apelora blocks TRUNCATE on the audit table; the same care applies to logs on the servers.

    3. Find out what was done

    Filter the audit trail by actor, then read in time order. The actions that matter most:

    QuestionLook for
    Did they get in?auth.oidc.signed_in, auth.password.succeeded and auth.password.failed, auth.directory.*, auth.locked, auth.refused (a disabled account)
    Did they try things they could not do?access.denied, session.denied, refused approvals
    Did they give themselves or others more access?role.assign, role.revoke, role.create, group.member.add, emergency_access.request and emergency_access.grant, token.create (and token.create_admin), personal_token.create, service_identity.create
    Did they get a shell?session.request, session.open, session.close (instance, source address, duration)
    Did they change or remove workloads?application.deploy.requested, application.operation, application.delete, application.volume.delete, application.volume.restore, instance.create/start/stop/delete/move, cluster.member.evacuate, cluster.member.remove
    Did they read data out?audit.exported, support bundle downloads
    Did they change security settings?sign-in provider, directory, policy and approval-rule changes

    For each terminal session, the instance's own logs and the changes that follow it are what shows the effect. If the instance may have been altered, rebuild it from the design rather than trusting it: redeploy, and restore its volumes from a backup taken before the incident (storage-incident.md).

    4. Recover

    1. Rotate what the person or script could reach: service tokens, their personal tokens, the Incus certificates if the control plane host was involved (certificates.md), the identity provider's client secret and any webhook secrets (the alert receiver's signing secret lives on the workers).
    2. If the control plane host itself is suspect, restore onto a clean host from Apelora's database backup and re-issue every credential (apelora-database-backup.md); the old host's secrets must be treated as lost.
    3. Re-enable what you turned off one thing at a time, and watch the audit trail as you do.
    4. Make sure a person who needs access again gets it through a normal assignment, with the approval that production asks for, not a shortcut.

    5. Learn

    • Write what happened, when, how it was found and how long containment took.
    • Close the gap: a shorter session limit (APELORA_CONSOLE_IDLE_SECS, APELORA_CONSOLE_MAX_SECS), an approval rule or MFA requirement under Access policies, a narrower role, fewer legacy tokens.
    • Add an entry to LESSONS-LEARNED.md if something non-obvious went wrong, and correct this runbook with what you verified.
